# M3.3A2 — P3: Frozen-vs-Extended behavioural parity

**Scope.** Prove that swapping `yaAGC.wasm` for `yaAGC-ext.wasm` — without
invoking any new hardware-interface export — does not alter legacy AGC
behaviour. P3 does **not** promote the extended artifact, modify the
production Worker, or begin monitor-mode integration.

## 1. HW-I/O ABI bump to v2 (pre-parity amendment)

Adding the explicit trace-arming exports `agc_out_trace_set_enabled` /
`agc_out_trace_enabled` (introduced by the dormancy correction) changes the
extension surface even though *armed* trace semantics are unchanged from v1.
Extension identity was bumped before parity:

- `agc_ext_version()` → `"ddc65e7be+apollo-browser-hwio-v2"`
- `agc_hwio_version()` → `2`
- v2 adds explicit trace arming (default **disabled**):
  - `agc_out_trace_set_enabled(uint32_t)` → previous value
  - `agc_out_trace_enabled(void)` → 0 unless armed
- Armed trace semantics are unchanged from v1.

## 2. Parity harness

`src/sim/agc/__tests__/hwioParity.test.ts` loads both artifacts directly
against identical `env.memory` and identical WASI stubs, bypassing
`AgcCoreAdapter` and the Worker. Both cores receive:

- The same Luminary099 rope bytes (`public/ropes/Luminary099.bin`,
  SHA-256 `1f5326e0…f40e`, 73 728 B) via `malloc + set_fixed + free`.
- The same `cpu_reset()` and the same input priming
  (`packet_write(0o32, 1<<13)`, `packet_write(0o15, 0)`).
- The same `cpu_step(n)` schedule and the same `packet_write` events
  (channel + value) at the same points in that schedule.

Trace arming and every hardware-input export are **never invoked** during
the harness. Every scenario continuously asserts
`agc_out_trace_enabled() === 0` and `agc_out_trace_dropped() === 0`, and the
suite finishes with an explicit unarmed drain that returns 0.

### Compared surfaces (equality required)

- The full ordered `packet_read()` stream: channel, value, and position.
- Erasable-memory contents (all 2048 words, 15-bit) at every checkpoint.
- Cumulative `cpu_step` totals (bookkept per core, compared implicitly by the
  step schedules being identical).
- TIME1 (0o24) and TIME2 (0o25) after the long-idle scenario.

### Not compared

Arbitrary linear-memory pages, pointer addresses, extension-only symbols and
their identity strings, artifact size / SHA, and WASI import shape.

Total: **6 scenarios**, **≈5.4M cpu_steps** compared, **8 erasable
checkpoints** compared, packet streams compared after every schedule
segment (~150 segments across the suite).

- V35 parity: identical packet streams and erasable state through the
  authoritative-peak transient and teardown.
- V16 N65 parity: identical erasable state and packet stream at mid and
  late monitor checkpoints (MET counter advancing on both cores).
- Dormancy: enforced continuously — no scenario ever observed
  `trace_enabled != 0` or `trace_dropped != 0`, and the sampler never
  mutated extension counters or trace storage.

## 4. First-divergence discipline

The comparator throws on the first packet or erasable mismatch and reports
scenario, segment, cumulative cpu_steps, last matching packet, first
differing packet, extension trace state, and erasable diffs. No mismatch
was observed in any scenario, so no divergence report was emitted.

## 5. Performance observation

`scripts/parity-perf.mjs` runs a fixed 1 000 000-step workload (20 chunks
of 50 000 with drains) seven times per artifact after a warm-up.

| Artifact | Median | Min | Max | Heap |
|----------|--------|-----|-----|------|
| frozen    | 31.6 ms | 28.7 ms | 32.8 ms | 320 KiB |
| extended  | 26.3 ms | 24.4 ms | 32.8 ms | 384 KiB |

The extended artifact is not slower than the frozen artifact under
dormant conditions; the small delta is within noise. Heap usage is
constant across runs on both cores — no growth. Nothing here blocks
promotion.
